  You're right in principle, and the codebase is partway there — but not where
  you're picturing. I measured the current 16k preset headless: 2.3 GB RSS, 5.9 
  s to load + warm up + render. Here's what that 2.3 GB is actually made of,
  because it tells you which half of your sentence is done and which isn't.

  What already exists (the "culled / LOD'd" half):
  - LOD — the near/far dot split in render.c: distant systems draw as one O(N)
  camera-relative point pass, one draw call. This is real and it's why 16k
  renders fine.
  - Simulation cull — the active region (ACTIVE_RADIUS_LY in main.c): only
  systems near the camera are stepped; the rest freeze.
  - Label cull — labels.c now uses a slot cache keyed to the active bodies, so
  labels follow you across the galaxy.

  What does not exist (the "dynamically loaded/unloaded" half):
  - Residency is static. Every one of the 16k bodies is fully instantiated in
  RAM from boot — full Body struct plus a 515 KB trail buffer (TRAIL_LEN=16384 ×
  double[3] + seg-lengths), including stars, which never render a trail
  (trails.c:230 skips them). It's demand-paged, so an untouched star trail costs
  address space, not RSS — that's the only reason 16k fits in 2.3 GB and not 8
  GB. But nothing ever unloads. Nothing streams in from disk as you approach.
  It's "load the whole JSON, keep it all forever."
  - O(n²) load loops. The centre-of-mass velocity correction in universe.c
  (~line 1011) is O(stars × bodies). Invisible at 16k, but it's part of that 5.9
  s and it would dominate at 100k+.
  - Collision is still index-capped to the first 128 body indices (ARCHITECTURE
  §8.1 "B2-collision") — it doesn't follow the camera.

  So today, count does still matter. Naively regenerating the preset with 372k
  Gaia stars would balloon VSZ to ~30 GB of calloc'd trail buffers (risking
  overcommit refusal on your 15 GB box) and make the O(n²) loader crawl — even
  though the renderer could draw them fine.

  To make count genuinely not matter, the missing pieces are, in order of 
  leverage:
  1. Don't allocate trails for stars — 96% of the bodies, zero trails rendered.

  per-body cost.

  color + magnitude, not a Body + 515 KB trail. Promote to a full Body only on
  entry to the active region (the starsys.c promotion hooks already gesture at
  this).
  3. Fix the O(n²) load loops so instantiation is linear.
  4. Then streaming residency (load/unload by camera) falls out naturally, and
  the far-field dot pass you already have is the LOD tier that draws them.

     #define STARBIN_MAGIC   0x4F4D5653u   /* 'OMVS' */
     #define STARBIN_VERSION 1u
     typedef struct { uint32_t magic, version, count, record_size; }
     StarBinHeader;
     typedef struct {
         uint64_t source_id;   /* → name (decimal string) */
         float    pos_ly[3];   /* ecliptic→GL frame, light-years (see §frame) */
         float    vel_kms[3];  /* system bulk velocity, km/s, GL frame */
         float    mass_kg;     /* stellar mass, kg */
         float    radius_km;   /* stellar radius, km */
         uint8_t  color[3];    /* display RGB 0..255 */
         uint8_t  _pad;
     } StarBinRecord;          /* 44 bytes; sizeof→48 (8-byte align) */
     record_size is written and validated against sizeof(StarBinRecord) on read,
     so ABI drift fails loudly instead of silently misparsing. float32 pos_ly is
     deliberate: worst-case resolution at 100 pc is ~1300 AU — astronomically
     irrelevant for point stars, and Body.pos is still double metres so the
     camera-relative float-cancellation invariant (ARCHITECTURE.md §units) is
     intact.
     372k records ≈ 18 MB.

● I have the full picture now. Here's the diagnosis — and yes, there's a lot to
  squeeze.

  Why the FPS is low

  It's the 262k stars, but not because the GPU can't draw them — 262k point
  sprites is trivial for any real GPU (it renders millions). The bottleneck is
  CPU/memory, and it's structural: several times per frame the renderer (and
  physics) scan all 278k bodies, even though ~262k of them are frozen catalog
  stars that never move.

  Per frame, over the full body array:
  - render.c:1824 — main pass: sqrt distance + visual_radius + projection, per
  body
  - render.c:2631 — near-dot classify
  - render.c:2805 — far-dot build: recomputes distance/fade/HDR-gain/pixel-size
  per star and rebuilds + re-uploads an ~8 MB vertex buffer every frame
  - render.c:2136/2898/2977 — three more full-body passes
  - main.c:1293 — physics scans all ~272k systems to pick the handful that are
  active

  That's roughly 6–8 full sweeps of a 167 MB body array per frame (278k × ~600
  bytes). Streaming ~1 GB/frame through memory single-threaded caps you at
  ~15–20 fps before the GPU does anything — which matches what you're seeing.
  Physics itself is fine (it only integrates active systems); the cost is these
  bookkeeping scans.

  The dumb part: the far stars are static. Their world positions never change,
  yet we recompute and re-upload all of them every frame. The dot shader takes
  camera-relative positions (a_pos = pos − camera, subtracted on the CPU), which

● Here's what it means, concretely.

  The "active region" model

  To run a galaxy in real time, the sim doesn't integrate every body every
  frame. Each frame it picks only the star systems within 2 light-years of the 
  camera (active_radius_ly = 2.0 in settings.c:21) and runs physics on just
  those. That's the for (a < n_active) loop in main.c — everything else is
  skipped entirely.

  A system outside that 2 ly bubble is "frozen": its physics_respa_* integration
  never runs, so its bodies' positions and velocities are simply never updated.
  The star stays at exactly the coordinates it loaded at. ARCHITECTURE.md puts
  it plainly (line 1277): "only systems within ACTIVE_RADIUS_LY of the camera 
  are stepped; distant systems freeze."

  Why that applies to almost all the catalog stars

  Your Gaia field spans 4 to ~300+ light-years. The active bubble is 2 ly. So
  from any given camera position, the overwhelming majority — essentially all
  262k catalog stars — sit outside the bubble and are frozen. They render as
  points, but the CPU never simulates them, and their world positions are
  constant frame to frame.

  This is deliberate and standard (Space Engine / Celestia do the same) — it's
  exactly what makes 278k bodies tractable at all. As you fly toward a star and
  it crosses inside 2 ly, it "thaws": it starts getting integrated, its planets
  (if any) begin orbiting. Fly away and it freezes again.

  Why that's the optimization opening

  Here's the mismatch: those 262k stars never move (physics correctly skips
  them), yet the renderer recomputes each one's screen distance, fade,
  brightness, and point size — and re-uploads the whole ~8 MB dot buffer — every
  single frame. We're paying full per-frame cost to redraw a field that, in
  world space, is completely static. The only thing changing is the camera.

  So "frozen catalog stars that never move" = they're outside the 2 ly
  simulation bubble, physics leaves their positions untouched, but the render
  path treats them as if they might have moved. That's the waste the static-VBO
  fix (#1) eliminates: build their dot geometry once, let the GPU handle the
  camera motion, and stop rebuilding 8 MB of unchanging star positions 60 times
  a second.
